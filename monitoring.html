Video monitoring

A monitor that watches a camera feed frame by frame and raises one of four levels: NOMINAL, WATCH, ALARM or TRIP. It is built to catch the scene that is heading towards failure, to stay quiet through the ordinary changes that fool simple detectors, and to tell a broken camera apart from a dangerous scene.

This page shows what the monitor does and how well it does it. The method, the source code and the trained models are not published. Every number below was measured on synthetic video with known ground truth, not on real plant footage.

EWMA and CUSUM catch everything, but they alarm on about half the quiet scenes and on every scene cut, so an operator learns to ignore them. Motion detection and background subtraction stay quieter but miss hazards and all slow drift. None of the four can say "the camera is broken" rather than "the scene is dangerous". The Coherent Thread monitor's 5 false alarms on quiet scenes each come with the reason they fired, shown for review.

Real time and frame rate

Fed one 96×72 frame at a time at 30 fps, across all 14 scenes, the monitor needs 1.8 ms per frame at the median against a budget of 33 ms. Its slowest 1% of frames take up to 64 ms and the single slowest 119 ms, so it can fall a few frames behind for a moment and then catch up. Larger frames have not been measured on this page.

A vision-language model in the loop

An open 2-billion-parameter vision-language model (Qwen3-VL-2B, run locally on CPU) can be asked to investigate a scene: it calls measurement tools, then gives a verdict. It advises, and it has no authority: it can raise the level to ALARM at most, and it can never lower a level or clear a trip. 28 held-out episodes:

SetupCorrect verdictClaims backed by a measurementSafeTool callsTime per episode
Base model50%0%100%5.1107 s
Fine-tuned68%21%100%3.789 s
Base model, guarded82%100%100%2.777 s
Fine-tuned, guarded86%100%100%2.659 s

"Guarded" means the investigation rules are enforced, not only scored. The guard is the largest single gain: from 50% to 82% correct, with 9 episodes gained and none lost (sign test p = 0.002). Fine-tuning adds a smaller step on top, which 28 episodes cannot yet confirm (p = 0.5). At about a minute per episode, this is a second opinion beside the live monitor, not part of it.

We also asked the same model one plain question, "is the view blocked?", on 40 frames. It answered yes on all of them, blocked or not. The monitor therefore gives its answer zero weight. What an answer is worth is measured before it counts, and here it is worth nothing.

Read before relying on it

All results above come from synthetic video with known ground truth. The monitor has not been validated on real footage, has no safety certification and no independent audit. The frames are small, the clip counts are small (8 per scene, 28 investigation episodes), and the weak spots in amber are real. Use it as one layer among several, never as the only barrier between a fault and a catastrophe.
